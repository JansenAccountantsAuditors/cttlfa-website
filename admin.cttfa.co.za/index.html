CTTLFA Admin Centre — full guide

This is the single guide to everything in the Admin Centre. It explains what each module does, how to reach it, who may use it, and where the external tools sit. The Admin Centre is the association's one online workspace for the Management Committee and office. You launch each function from a card on the Home page; the left sidebar always gives you Home, Users and Help.

Getting around

The Home page is the launcher. Each function is a card; press Open to go into it. The left sidebar stays with you throughout and holds three links: Home (back to the launcher), Users (who may sign in) and Help (this page).

Some modules open with a second row of tabs at the top of the page. Voting & Meetings has Meetings, Accreditation, Compliance, Motions, Live & results and Audit. Website Admin has its own row too — Notices, News, Committee, Clubs, Sponsors, Downloads, Key dates, Gallery, Live data and Settings. Those sub-tabs belong to that module and show only while you are in it.

Quick navigation. A round gold + button sits in the bottom-right corner of every page. Press it for a short menu that jumps straight to any module — Home, Voting, Registrations, Website Analytics, Website Admin, Fincom, the live site — or back to the top of a long page, so you are not scrolling to get around.
External tools open in a new browser tab. Fixtures (LeagueRepublic), the discipline dashboard and the live public website open separately, so the Admin Centre stays open behind them for you to come back to.

Signing in

Only people on the Mancom allow-list can sign in. The first time, choose First time, set password, enter your approved email and set your own password (at least eight characters). After that, sign in with the same email and password. If you forget it, an administrator can confirm your email is on the list; a password reset can then be arranged. Never share a login. Each person uses their own so the record shows who did what.

Access levels

There are four user types, set per person on the Users page:

  1. Administrator. Sees and edits every module (voting and meetings, registrations, website admin, Fincom) and manages the Users list. Administrators can change other people's access.
  2. Staff. Sees everything, but can edit only the areas an administrator ticks for them (for example Website Admin and Voting). Everything else is view only.
  3. Fincom. Sees only the Fincom Dashboard, and only to view it. Nothing else in the Admin Centre is shown to a Fincom user. Use this for Finance Committee members who need the packs but not the rest.
  4. Viewer. Sees everything, view only. Cannot change anything. Use this for members who need sight of the information without editing rights.

Editable areas for Staff. For a Staff user, an administrator ticks which areas they may edit (Voting, Registrations, Website Admin, Discipline, Fincom, Club Debtors, Fixtures, Live site). A Staff user still sees every area; the ticks decide only what they can change. Anything not ticked is view only for them.

A change to someone's access takes effect the next time they sign in. An administrator cannot remove their own administrator rights or deactivate their own account, which prevents an accidental lock-out.

Voting & Meetings

What it is: the tool that runs AGMs, SGMs, General and Mancom meetings. It handles delegate accreditation, club compliance, motions, live secret ballots on delegates' own phones, quorum, results and the formal record.

Where: Home → Voting & Meetings. Who: administrators run it; viewers can watch. The full meeting-day walkthrough is further down this page under Voting: full meeting-day guide.

Note: delegates themselves vote on the separate public site vote.cttfa.co.za, using a QR or six-character code issued at the door. That delegate site is the only place voting still appears; the old voteadmin site is retired and now redirects to this Admin Centre.

Registrations & Clubs

What it is: the association-wide registration picture, built on the SAFA two-year card cycle. It shows which cycle we are in, how many members are registered, who they are (senior, junior, foreign), how many are returning versus new, when in the year cards are loaded, a per-club breakdown, and per-club profiles that pull in live teams, fixtures, results and form from the public website.

Where: Home → Registrations & Clubs. Who: read-only, administrators only, because it holds member-level data.

Look up a member. At the top of the page is a search box that reads the whole registered roll. Type a SAFA number, surname or first name to find any player, referee, coach or official, and click the result to see their full card history across every cycle — useful for verifying on the spot whether someone is registered and where. Identity numbers are never shown or exported (Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013).

Read the picture, not just the numbers. Below the search the page gives you a plain-language read of the season (“what the numbers mean”), doughnut charts of the membership by category and returning-versus-new, a running total of how the cycle has built up, the monthly registration pattern, and a ranking of the largest clubs. Every figure, slice and bar is clickable: click it to list the members behind it and export that list to CSV or PDF. Click a club, or a bar in the top-clubs chart, to open its full profile.

Compare cycles. Use the cycle selector to compare the current cycle against the previous one, and watch the countdown to the next card cycle so re-carding is planned in good time.

Website Admin

What it is: where the office edits the content of the public site (www.cttfa.co.za) without touching code or asking anyone to redeploy. It now lives fully inside the Admin Centre, with its own row of sub-tabs. The public site reads this content and updates on the next page load.

The sub-tabs:

  • Notices — short dated messages shown as a strip on the public site.
  • News — uploaded graphics or posters for the home and news pages.
  • Committee — the office bearers shown publicly (phone and internal contact stay private).
  • Clubs — the full club record for all 55 clubs: name and membership type, ground and suburb, a map pin you set by searching an address or dragging a marker (the latitude and longitude fill in for you), honours, website and Facebook, and the LeagueRepublic names the club plays under. A Fetch from LeagueRepublic button pulls the live team list, matches names to the club automatically, and lets you map any it does not recognise with one click. This mapping is what lets the public site show each club’s fixtures and results.
  • Sponsors, Downloads, Key dates, Gallery — the sponsor credit, the document library, the events list and the photo albums.
  • Live data — see below.
  • Settings — the Google Maps key used by the club map picker.

Live data (refresh the live site). The Live data tab shows when the public site last refreshed its match data from LeagueRepublic, and when the registration figures were last loaded. The match data updates automatically about every hour. Straight after you capture results, press Refresh live data now to push the update within a few minutes — it opens the sync on GitHub, where you press Run workflow. Re-read feed reloads the latest into the Admin Centre, for example before you map club names on the Clubs tab.

Where: Home → Website Admin. Who: administrators, and any Staff user given Website Admin edit rights. Viewers see the content but cannot change it. The separate old websiteadmin site has fallen away.

Website Analytics

What it is: a live view of how the public site (www.cttfa.co.za) is being used — how many people visit, whether they arrive from Google, social media or a direct link, which pages they read most, which countries they are in, and what they do on the site. It reads straight from Google Analytics, so it is always current, and you choose the period you want to look at.

How to read it (for anyone). The three headline tiles are the plain story: Active users is how many different people used the site, New users is how many were there for the first time, and Sessions is how many separate visits there were (one person can visit several times). The line shows how visitors rise and fall day by day. The bars show where people come from and which pages they open. The map shows where in the world they are — for us, almost entirely South Africa. Change the date box at the top to look at a day, a month or the whole season.

Where: Home → Website Analytics. Who: every signed-in user can see it; there is nothing to edit. It carries no member or personal information, only visitor counts.

Discipline, Referees & Cards

What it is: the yellow and red card tracker, suspensions, referee appointments and administrative rulings.

Where: Home → Discipline. This opens the existing dashboard at dash.cttlfa.com in a new tab. Who: administrators. A rebuild into the Admin Centre look and feel is planned, starting with read-only views.

Fincom Dashboard

What it is: the Finance Committee operating dashboard, now hosted inside the Admin Centre. It covers the Fincom mandate and scope, the KPI summary, the 2026 budget and its monthly phasing, debtors, compliance, the work plan and meeting rhythm, and a document library holding the monthly Mancom packs, the budgets, the finance policy manual and the debtor-system review.

Where: Home → Fincom Dashboard, which opens the gated page inside the Admin Centre (no separate site). Who: administrators and any user given Fincom area access on the Mancom users page. Everyone else does not see the card at all.

Security: the figures and documents are not on any public address. They are served only to a signed-in user who has Fincom access; the document links are secure, single-session links that expire after an hour. The old public cttfafincom.tiiny.site version has been retired in favour of this.

Club Debtors

What it is: the live club debtor dashboard, drawn from Sage. It shows each club's balance and ageing, who has paid and who is behind, the constitutional collection cadence, the statement and reminder letters, and the out-of-compliance register for Mancom. Balances and the transaction ledger refresh from Sage on demand, and every figure ties back to the club statement.

Where: Home → Club Debtors. Who: everyone signed in can see it. Editing, meaning logging notes and actions, setting the working status, placing or clearing out of compliance, editing club contacts, running correspondence and sending emails, is limited to administrators and to Staff who have been given Club Debtors edit access on the Users page. Everyone else sees it view only.

Overdue and out of compliance: only amounts in the 60 day and older buckets count as overdue, so a club is not treated as behind for the current month's invoice. Out of compliance is shown as an indicator of who has reached the trigger, not a statement of fact. A club is only actually out of compliance once you place it there, which is a governed decision recorded against the club with the date.

Sending emails: the email layer is in test mode until Mancom approves go-live. In test mode every email, whether a structured statement or reminder or a free-form message you write from the club profile, is delivered to the Treasurer only and not to the club, with a copy kept in the treasurer mailbox. Once live, the same emails go to each club's address on file, copied to the treasurer and ops mailboxes. Every email issued is kept in the club's correspondence trail for the audit file.

Fixtures & Results

What it is: live league tables, fixtures and results across every division, run on LeagueRepublic.

Where: Home → Fixtures & Results. The Open link goes to the public LeagueRepublic site (cttfass.leaguerepublic.com), which needs no login for viewing. The LR admin login link goes to the LeagueRepublic sign-in page for managing fixtures, results and referee appointments.

On passwords: no LeagueRepublic password is stored in the Admin Centre. On the office machine, save the LeagueRepublic login in the browser's own password manager once, and it fills in on the sign-in page from then on. For accountability, prefer separate LeagueRepublic logins per person over one shared login.

Live Website

What it is: the public site (www.cttfa.co.za) exactly as members and the public see it: fixtures, clubs, news and more. Use it to check how a change looks once it is live. Where: Home → Live Website, opens in a new tab.

Managing users

Open Users from the sidebar. Administrators see a management view; everyone else sees the list only.

  1. Add a person. Fill in their name, email and (optionally) title, choose the user type (Viewer, Staff, Fincom or Administrator), and press Add to allow-list. They then sign in with that email and set their own password.
  2. Change access. Use the drop-down in the User type column to change a person's type. For a Staff user, tick the areas they may edit in the next column.
  3. Suspend or restore. Use Deactivate to stop someone signing in without deleting their record, and Activate to restore them.

All changes apply the next time that person signs in. You cannot deactivate or demote your own administrator account.

Voting & Meetings: full meeting-day guide

This is the full guide for the people running a meeting. Delegates vote from their own phones using a code you give them at the door. Nothing is installed, they just open a link. The steps below follow the order you actually work in on the day. Hover over any button on the other tabs to see a short reminder of what it does.

The day at a glance Before the meeting Compliance Accreditation Codes: QR and SMS Quorum Motions Running a vote Projector Results and records How delegates vote Common questions

1The day at a glance

A meeting runs in the same order every time. Do the first three before delegates start voting, then repeat the vote step for each motion.

  1. Pick the meeting. On the Meetings tab, select the meeting you are running. Its name shows in the grey bar at the top of every tab so you always know which one you are on.
  2. Set compliance. On the Compliance tab, confirm which clubs are compliant and press Save compliance. Do this before you start accrediting.
  3. Accredit delegates at the door. On the Accreditation tab, find each delegate, capture their cell and consent, and press Check in and issue code. Hand them their QR or send the code by SMS.
  4. Prepare the motions. On the Motions tab, type up each motion. You can do this days ahead.
  5. Run each vote. On the Live & results tab, press Start on a motion, let the timer run, then read the result. Repeat for the next motion.
  6. Keep the records. Download the attendance PDF and the results PDF for the minutes.
Two laptops is fine. Two people can accredit at the same time on separate laptops. Only the chairperson's laptop should start and close the actual votes, to keep control in one place.

2Before the meeting: create it

On the Meetings tab, fill in the name, type (AGM, SGM, General or Mancom), date and venue, then press Create meeting. You can add a Google Drive link so delegates can reach the meeting documents. The meeting you create becomes the working meeting straight away.

You can create the meeting well in advance and set everything up quietly before the doors open. Come back on the day, select it from the list, and carry on.

3Compliance: who is allowed to vote

A club must be in good standing to be accredited and to vote. The Compliance tab is where you record that for this meeting.

  1. Open the Compliance tab. Every club is listed and starts as Compliant.
  2. For any club that is not in good standing, change its dropdown to Not compliant. The row turns red.
  3. Press Save compliance to confirm.

A club marked not compliant cannot be checked in on the Accreditation tab, and its votes are left out of the voting strength. You can change the list and save again as many times as you need up to the moment the first motion is started.

It locks once voting begins. The instant you start the first motion, the compliance list locks so the basis of the vote cannot be changed underneath a result. If you need to fix compliance after that, it can only be done before any vote is opened, so check it carefully first. A locked list shows a small locked tag.

4Accreditation: checking delegates in

This is the busy part at the door. For each delegate:

  1. Find the person. In the big search box, type their SAFA number or surname. Pick them from the list and their club and SAFA number fill in on their own. If they are not on the roll, just type their name in the delegate name field to add them by hand.
  2. Narrow to a club (optional). If two people share a surname, type the club name in the Club filter first to search inside that club only. Leave it blank to search everyone.
  3. Capture the cell number so you can SMS the code, and tick the consent box. The consent line covers holding the name and cell under POPIA.
  4. Press Check in and issue code. The QR code and six-character code pop up.
Votes are worked out for you. A full member club has two votes. The first delegate from that club is given both votes. If a second delegate from the same club checks in, the system automatically splits it to one vote each. A third is blocked and shows Subscribed. An associate club has one vote and one delegate only. You never add or split votes by hand, the box under "Votes allocated" tells you exactly what this delegate will carry.

Made a mistake? Use Remove on the checked-in list to take a delegate out and return their vote to the club. Use New code to cancel a code and issue a fresh one, for example if someone changes phones.

5Getting the code to the delegate

Each delegate needs their own code on their own phone. You have two ways to give it to them, and you can use both.

  1. QR code. When you check someone in, a QR shows on screen. They open their phone camera, point it at the QR, and tap the link. It opens the vote site with their code already filled in. You can show it again any time with the QR button.
  2. SMS. Press the SMS button on their row and the system texts them the code and the link. This needs a cell number captured. Good for delegates who are not standing at the desk.
One phone per code. A code locks to the first phone that opens it. It cannot be used on a second phone, which stops one code being passed around to vote twice. If a delegate genuinely needs to move to a new phone, issue a New code.

6Quorum

The live quorum panel on the Accreditation tab checks both limbs the Constitution requires (Articles 6.2.5 and 6.3.3): more than half of the clubs present, and more than half of Mancom present. Clubs present is counted for you as delegates check in. Type the Mancom numbers into the two small boxes. When both limbs are met the panel reads QUORATE. Do not open a vote until it does.

7Motions: preparing the questions

On the Motions tab you write up each thing the meeting will vote on. A motion has a title, a plain instruction shown on every phone, a pass rule, who may vote, how abstentions are treated, and a timer. Press Add motion to save it as a draft. It is not open for voting yet.

You can have many motions in one day. An AGM or a set of constitutional amendments may have several, up to about ten. Prepare them all in advance so you are not typing while the room waits. On the day you simply start them one after another. Keep the number sensible, a long list of votes tires the room and the wording of each must be clear on its own.

Choosing the pass rule. Use Simple majority for ordinary business (more For than Against). Use Two-thirds for constitutional amendments and anything the Constitution reserves to a special majority. Use Custom % only when a specific threshold is required. For Who may vote, leave it on Full + Associate unless the matter is reserved to full member clubs. For Abstentions, "Set aside" ignores them; "Count in base" makes an abstention effectively count against the motion, so choose deliberately.

8Running a vote on the day

Everything for the live vote is on the Live & results tab, and the Start and Close buttons also sit on the Motions tab.

  1. When the chairperson calls the motion, press Start. Every delegate's phone switches to the ballot for that motion, and the countdown begins.
  2. Watch the timer and the live count of votes in. The running Yes/No split is never shown, the ballot is secret.
  3. The vote closes on its own when the timer hits zero. You can also press Close to end it early once everyone has voted.
  4. Anyone accredited who did not vote is counted as an abstain. This is deliberate, silence is recorded, not ignored.
  5. Start the next motion and repeat. Only one motion is ever open at a time.

9The projector (room view)

The room view is a big screen for the hall so everyone can follow the vote without looking at a laptop. It is a separate page you open when you need it.

  1. On the Live & results tab, press Open room view. It opens in a new browser tab.
  2. Drag that tab onto the projector screen, then press Full screen at the top right of the room view to fill the whole screen.
  3. While a vote is open the screen shows the motion and a large countdown. It never shows the running tally, so the ballot stays secret.
  4. When the vote closes the screen shows CARRIED or NOT CARRIED with the Yes, No and Abstain totals.
  5. To take it down, press Full screen again (or Esc) to leave full screen, then Close to end the room view, and close the browser tab.

The room view updates on its own every few seconds. You control the vote from the admin laptop as usual, the projector just mirrors the state.

10Results and records for the minutes

As each motion closes, its result appears in the results table on the Live & results tab with Yes, No, Abstain and the outcome. For the meeting file:

  1. Attendance PDF on the Accreditation tab gives the full attendance and voting register: every delegate, club, votes carried and code.
  2. Results PDF on the Live tab gives every closed motion with its totals and outcome. This is the formal voting record.
  3. The Audit tab keeps a read-only trail of every accreditation, removal, compliance change and motion start or close, with who did it and when. It is your evidence if a result is ever questioned.

11What the delegate does on their phone

This is the whole delegate experience, so you can explain it to a club in one breath: open the link, vote when it opens, done. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

  1. They scan the QR you show them, or tap the link in the SMS, or go to vote.cttfa.co.za and type their six-character code.
  2. Their name and club show, so they can check it is right. Then they wait, the phone says voting has not opened yet.
  3. When you start a motion, their phone shows the motion and three buttons: Yes, No, Abstain. They tap one and confirm.
  4. Once the vote closes, their phone shows whether it was carried. That is it.
Share it with a club ahead of time. On the club's row you can send a short SMS that explains, in plain language, how their delegate votes on the day. It is the same simple steps as above. Send it a day or two before so clubs arrive knowing what to do.

Common questions

A delegate says their code will not work.Check they are using it on the first phone that opened it, codes lock to one phone. If they changed phones, press New code on their row to issue a fresh one. The old one then stops working.
We accredited the wrong person.Press Remove on their row. Their vote goes back to the club and, if the club was split, it re-combines. Then accredit the correct delegate.
A club is short and shows "Subscribed".That club has already used all its votes (two for a full club, one for an associate). Remove a delegate first if you need to swap who holds the vote.
Can we change compliance after starting a vote?No. It locks at the first Start to protect the result. Set it correctly before the first motion opens.
The vote closed before everyone voted.The timer closes it automatically. Anyone who did not vote is recorded as an abstain, which is the correct treatment. Set a longer timer on the motion if the room needs more time.
Why can I not see who is winning while the vote is open?The ballot is secret. Only the count of votes cast is shown live, never the Yes/No split. The result appears when the vote closes.

Send the guide to a club by SMS

Text a club's secretary a short plain-English guide on how their delegate votes, so they arrive on the day knowing what to do. Send it a day or two before the meeting.

The message reads: "CTTLFA voting guide for [club]: your delegate votes on their own phone at the meeting. At the desk we give them a QR or 6-character code. When a motion opens they tap Yes, No or Abstain and confirm. One phone per code, secret ballot. More: https://vote.cttfa.co.za"
